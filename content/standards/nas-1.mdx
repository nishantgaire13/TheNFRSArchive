## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

Every business needs to show its financial health in a clear, consistent way. Think of financial statements like a report card for a company — they tell stakeholders how much money the company has, how much it earned, and where the cash went. Just as every school in Nepal follows a common grading system so parents can compare performance, NAS 1 ensures every company follows a common format so investors, banks, and regulators can compare businesses.

NAS 1 requires a **complete set of financial statements**: a balance sheet (statement of financial position), a profit and loss statement (statement of profit or loss and OCI), a statement of changes in equity, a cash flow statement, and notes. If a Kathmandu shopkeeper and a large bank like Nabil Bank both follow NAS 1, anyone reading their accounts can find the same types of information in the same places.

The standard also lays down general features every set of financial statements must have: they must be prepared on a **going concern** basis (assuming the business will continue operating), use the **accrual basis** of accounting (recording transactions when they occur, not when cash moves), and present items consistently from one period to the next.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 1 establishes the overall framework for general purpose financial statements — those intended for users who cannot demand customised reports. It prescribes the minimum structure and content, including a complete set of financial statements, general features such as fair presentation, going concern, accrual accounting, materiality, and aggregation, and guidance on the statement of financial position, the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, and the notes.

For Nepali entities, NAS 1 intersects heavily with regulatory formatting requirements. NRB prescribes specific templates for banks and financial institutions, while SEBON mandates quarterly and annual reporting formats for listed companies. These regulatory templates must still comply with NAS 1's principles — when a conflict arises, the entity discloses the departure and its impact.

NAS 1 is fully aligned with IAS 1. The standard requires comparative information for the preceding period as a minimum, and a third balance sheet when an entity applies an accounting policy retrospectively or makes a retrospective restatement under NAS 8.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 1 operationalises the Conceptual Framework's qualitative characteristics at the presentation layer. Fair presentation (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="15" />) requires faithful representation of transactions in accordance with the definitions and recognition criteria in the Framework — it is not merely compliance with standards but substance over form. The "true and fair override" (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="19-22" />) permits departure from a standard in extremely rare circumstances where compliance would be misleading.

The single-statement versus two-statement approach to profit or loss and OCI (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="81A" />) remains a practical choice. Most Nepali banks present a single statement, while manufacturing entities tend to use the two-statement approach. The classification of OCI items between those that will be reclassified to profit or loss (e.g., FVOCI debt instruments under NFRS 9, foreign currency translation under NAS 21) and those that will not (e.g., FVOCI equity instruments, revaluation surplus under NAS 16, remeasurements of defined benefit plans under NAS 19) is a frequent source of error.

Nepal's adoption of NFRS in 2075 BS exposed presentation gaps: many entities had never prepared OCI statements or third balance sheets. The transition period saw significant restatements, particularly for BFIs adjusting for NFRS 9 expected credit losses.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope &amp; key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 1 applies to **all general purpose financial statements** — the accounts a company publishes for shareholders, lenders, regulators, and the public. It does not apply to interim financial reports (those are covered by NAS 34) or to condensed financial statements.

**Key terms:**
- **Financial statements** — The complete package of reports: balance sheet, income statement, equity statement, cash flow statement, and notes.
- **Going concern** — The assumption that the business will keep running for at least the next 12 months.
- **Accrual basis** — Recording sales when made and expenses when incurred, not when cash is received or paid.
- **Materiality** — Information is material if leaving it out could influence the decisions of users.
- **Other comprehensive income (OCI)** — Gains and losses not included in the profit and loss statement, such as revaluation gains on land.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 1 applies to all general purpose financial statements prepared in accordance with NFRS (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="2" />). It does not apply to interim financial reporting under NAS 34, though interim reports must be consistent with the annual presentation. The standard does not prescribe formats for entities applying NFRS for SMEs.

Key technical definitions:
- **Other comprehensive income (OCI)** — Items of income and expense not recognised in profit or loss as required or permitted by other NFRS. Examples: revaluation surplus changes (NAS 16), actuarial gains/losses on defined benefit plans (NAS 19), fair value changes on FVOCI financial assets (NFRS 9).
- **Current vs non-current** — NAS 1 requires classification of assets and liabilities as current or non-current unless a liquidity-based presentation provides more reliable and relevant information (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="60" />). BFIs in Nepal typically use a mixed presentation.
- **Offsetting** — Assets and liabilities, or income and expenses, shall not be offset unless required or permitted by an NFRS (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="32" />).

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The scope interaction between NAS 1 and sector-specific regulations creates practical tension. NRB's Unified Directives prescribe BFI balance sheet formats that differ from the IAS 1 illustrative guidance — yet <ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="57" /> only prescribes minimum line items, not a mandatory sequence. Thus NRB formats can comply with NAS 1 provided all minimum line items are present and the classification principles (current/non-current or liquidity) are consistently applied.

Edge cases requiring careful judgment include: (a) the third statement of financial position requirement when NAS 8 retrospective restatement is triggered — entities often omit this; (b) the distinction between changes in accounting policy (retrospective under NAS 8) and changes in presentation (reclassification of comparatives under <ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="41" />); and (c) materiality judgments for aggregation and disaggregation of line items — particularly for conglomerates with diverse operations where over-aggregation obscures meaningful information.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

NAS 1 does not itself establish recognition criteria — those come from individual standards (e.g., NAS 16 for PPE, NFRS 9 for financial instruments, NFRS 15 for revenue). Instead, NAS 1 requires that recognised items be presented and classified appropriately in the financial statements. The standard mandates that the complete set of financial statements be prepared at least annually (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="36" />), and when the reporting period changes, the entity must disclose the reason and the fact that amounts are not entirely comparable.

In Nepal, the fiscal year runs from 1 Shrawan to 31 Ashad (mid-July to mid-July). All entities following NFRS prepare annual statements aligned to this fiscal year. NRB requires BFIs to also publish unaudited quarterly results.

## Measurement

NAS 1 does not prescribe measurement bases — those are determined by individual standards (historical cost, fair value, NRV, present value, etc.). However, NAS 1 requires that the measurement bases used be disclosed in the significant accounting policies note. The standard also requires that when a third balance sheet is presented due to retrospective restatement under NAS 8, the entity is not required to present related notes for that opening balance sheet, though it must disclose the nature of the restatement.

## Presentation &amp; disclosure

NAS 1 is fundamentally a presentation and disclosure standard. Key requirements include: a complete set of five financial statements plus notes; identification of each statement with entity name, whether individual or consolidated, reporting period, currency, and level of rounding; minimum line items on the face of each statement (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="54" /> for financial position, <ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="82" /> for profit or loss); and a structured notes section starting with a statement of compliance with NFRS, followed by significant accounting policies, then supporting information for line items in the order they appear on the face of the statements.

Entities must also disclose judgments made in applying accounting policies that have the most significant effect on amounts recognised (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="122" />) and sources of estimation uncertainty that carry a significant risk of material adjustment within the next financial year (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="125" />). In Nepal, common disclosures under these paragraphs include loan impairment judgments for BFIs, useful life estimates for hydropower assets, and going concern assessments for hotel companies during periods of political instability.
