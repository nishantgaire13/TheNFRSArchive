## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

When Nepal decided that companies should follow international-quality accounting rules (NFRS), businesses had to switch from their old accounting methods (Nepal GAAP) to the new system. NFRS 1 is the rulebook for making this switch.

Think of it like moving from an old filing system to a new one. You need to set a "starting point" — an **opening NFRS balance sheet** — where you restate all your assets, liabilities, and equity as if you had always used NFRS. Nepal adopted NFRS on 1 Shrawan 2075 BS (16 July 2018), but the transition happened in phases: big listed companies (Class A) first, then medium ones (Class B), then smaller entities (Class C).

NFRS 1 gives some relief. Certain adjustments are mandatory (you must do them), and others are optional (you can choose to simplify the transition). For example, many Nepali companies used the **deemed cost exemption** — instead of figuring out what their land was worth when they first bought it decades ago, they could use the fair value at the transition date as the new starting cost. This was especially helpful because Kathmandu Valley land values have increased 5-10 times over the past 15 years.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 1 provides the framework for an entity's first set of financial statements prepared under Nepal Financial Reporting Standards. The standard requires a clear **date of transition** — the beginning of the earliest period for which an entity presents full comparative information under NFRS. The entity prepares an opening NFRS statement of financial position at this date.

The general principle is full retrospective application of all NFRS effective at the reporting date of the entity's first NFRS financial statements. However, the standard provides two categories of relief: **mandatory exceptions** (areas where retrospective application is prohibited, such as derecognition of financial assets/liabilities, hedge accounting, estimates, and non-controlling interests) and **optional exemptions** (areas where retrospective application is permitted but not required, such as deemed cost, business combinations, cumulative translation differences, and borrowing costs).

Nepal's phased NFRS adoption is still ongoing. Class A entities (banks, insurance companies, large listed entities) transitioned first, followed by Class B and Class C. Smaller entities and cooperatives are still transitioning, making NFRS 1 continually relevant. The most widely used optional exemption in Nepal has been the deemed cost exemption for property, plant, and equipment — particularly land — allowing entities to use fair value at the transition date as deemed cost.

NFRS 1 is fully aligned with IFRS 1.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NFRS 1 operates as a one-time standard with lasting consequences. The elections made at the date of transition — particularly deemed cost, business combination retrospectivity, and cumulative translation differences — permanently shape the entity's NFRS balance sheet. The standard's architecture separates mandatory exceptions (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="B7-B9" />) from optional exemptions (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="D1-D17" />) and requires disclosure of both in the first NFRS financial statements.

The deemed cost exemption (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="D5-D8B" />) has had the most significant balance sheet impact in Nepal. Entities with land acquired decades ago at nominal historical cost used fair value at the transition date — in many cases, recognising land values 5-10 times higher than previous GAAP carrying amounts. This created large adjustments to retained earnings (or a separate revaluation surplus) in the opening NFRS balance sheet. The deferred tax consequences of these deemed cost adjustments under NAS 12 were substantial, as the tax base remained at historical cost while the NFRS carrying amount jumped to fair value.

BFI transition posed unique challenges. Reclassifying financial instruments under NFRS 9 (replacing NAS 39), computing ECL provisions instead of incurred-loss provisions, recognising derivative positions not previously on balance sheet, and fair-valuing investment securities all required significant judgment. NRB provided transitional guidance allowing phased recognition of certain NFRS adjustments — creating a regulatory overlay that interacted with the pure NFRS 1 requirements.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope &amp; key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NFRS 1 applies to any entity preparing its first financial statements under NFRS — whether voluntarily or because regulators require it. Once you have adopted NFRS and issued your first NFRS financial statements, NFRS 1 no longer applies to you.

**Key terms:**
- **First NFRS financial statements** — The first annual financial statements in which the entity adopts NFRS with an explicit and unreserved statement of compliance.
- **Date of transition** — The beginning of the earliest period presented. If your first NFRS statements cover the year ending July 2019 with one year of comparatives, the transition date is 1 Shrawan 2074 (mid-July 2017).
- **Opening NFRS balance sheet** — The balance sheet prepared at the date of transition, restating everything under NFRS rules.
- **Deemed cost** — A fair value or revalued amount used as a substitute for historical cost at the transition date.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 1 applies to an entity when it issues its first annual financial statements containing an explicit and unreserved statement of compliance with NFRS (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="3" />). An entity does not become a first-time adopter simply because it previously applied some NFRS standards — the key is the compliance statement.

- **Previous GAAP** — the basis of accounting used immediately before NFRS adoption (in Nepal: Nepal Accounting Standards or Nepal GAAP issued by ASB Nepal).
- **Mandatory exceptions** — retrospective application is prohibited for: estimates (cannot use hindsight), derecognition of financial instruments, hedge accounting, non-controlling interests, and classification of financial instruments.
- **Optional exemptions** — retrospective application is permitted but not required for: deemed cost for PPE/intangibles/investment property, business combinations before transition date, cumulative translation differences, compound financial instruments, designation of previously recognised financial instruments, share-based payments, insurance contracts, borrowing costs, and others.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The estimates exception (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="B7-B9" />) is critical and frequently misunderstood. NFRS estimates at the transition date must be consistent with estimates made under previous GAAP for the same date, unless there is objective evidence those estimates were in error. This prevents entities from using hindsight to "improve" estimates — for example, an entity cannot revise its provision estimates at the transition date simply because the outcome is now known. However, if previous GAAP did not require an estimate that NFRS does (e.g., ECL under NFRS 9), new estimates must be made.

The interaction between NFRS 1's deemed cost exemption and NAS 12 creates complex deferred tax positions. When an entity uses fair value as deemed cost for land (no tax depreciation in Nepal for land), a taxable temporary difference arises if the deemed cost exceeds the tax base. Under NAS 12, this difference generates a deferred tax liability — but the initial recognition exemption in NAS 12 may apply if the land was not acquired in a business combination. Entities must carefully analyse whether the initial recognition exemption in <ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="15" /> applies at the NFRS transition date.

The business combination exemption (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="D1-D4" />) allows entities to not restate pre-transition business combinations. Given that NRB-directed bank mergers occurred under previous GAAP with different recognition and measurement rules, most BFIs elected this exemption. The consequence: goodwill from pre-transition mergers is carried at its previous GAAP amount, adjusted only for classification and recognition differences at the transition date.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

At the date of transition, an entity must recognise all assets and liabilities whose recognition is required by NFRS, derecognise assets or liabilities that NFRS does not permit, reclassify items from previous GAAP categories to appropriate NFRS categories, and apply NFRS measurement to all recognised assets and liabilities (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="10" />).

All adjustments resulting from the transition are recognised directly in retained earnings (or another category of equity, as appropriate) at the date of transition — not in profit or loss. This means the transition impact is a balance sheet adjustment, not an income statement event.

## Measurement

The general rule is full retrospective application of NFRS effective at the first reporting date. However, the optional exemptions significantly modify this. The deemed cost exemption allows entities to use fair value, previous GAAP revaluation, or an event-driven fair value as deemed cost for PPE, intangible assets, investment property, or right-of-use assets.

For financial instruments, NFRS 1 permits designation of financial assets and liabilities at fair value through profit or loss at the transition date (even if the entity would not meet the designation criteria at that date). Classification under NFRS 9's business model and SPPI tests is based on facts and circumstances at the transition date, not at original recognition.

## Presentation &amp; disclosure

An entity's first NFRS financial statements must include at least three statements of financial position (at the transition date, end of comparative period, and end of first NFRS period), two of each other statement (comprehensive income, changes in equity, cash flows), and related notes with comparative information (<ParaRef std="IFRS 1" para="21" />).

Key disclosures include: reconciliations of equity reported under previous GAAP to NFRS at the transition date and at the end of the latest period presented under previous GAAP, reconciliation of total comprehensive income under previous GAAP to NFRS for the latest period, explanation of material adjustments to the statement of financial position and comprehensive income, and disclosure of all optional exemptions used.
