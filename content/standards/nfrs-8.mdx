## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

Large companies often do many different things. A bank might have retail banking for everyday customers and corporate banking for big companies. A conglomerate might run hotels, manufacture cement, and generate hydropower. NFRS 8 says these companies must show results for each major part of their business separately — these parts are called **operating segments**.

Why does this matter? If Himalayan Bank reports only one combined profit number, you cannot tell whether retail banking is profitable while corporate banking is struggling. Segment reporting breaks the numbers apart so investors and regulators can see what is really going on inside the business.

NFRS 8 only applies to companies whose shares or debt instruments are traded on NEPSE or are in the process of listing.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 8 requires entities whose equity or debt securities are publicly traded (or in the process of being listed) to disclose financial information about their operating segments. The standard uses a **management approach** — segments are identified based on the internal reports regularly reviewed by the entity's chief operating decision maker (CODM) to allocate resources and assess performance.

For Nepali BFIs, typical segments include retail banking, corporate/commercial banking, treasury operations, and development banking (for those with development banking licenses). Conglomerates like CG Group or Chaudhary Group would report segments for each distinct business line. Hydropower companies with multiple projects may report by project or geographical region.

NFRS 8 is fully aligned with IFRS 8. The standard's management approach means segment information matches what management actually uses — not an artificial split for external reporting.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NFRS 8's management approach (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="5-10" />) represents a fundamental shift from the risks-and-rewards approach of the superseded IAS 14R. The CODM concept is a function, not a title — it refers to the person or group making operating decisions about resource allocation and performance assessment. In Nepali conglomerates, identifying the CODM can be complex where family patriarchs may make strategic decisions informally outside the documented board structure.

The aggregation criteria (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="12" />) — similar economic characteristics, similar nature of products/services, production processes, customer types, distribution methods, and regulatory environment — require significant judgment. Nepali BFIs must carefully consider whether development banking and commercial banking have "similar economic characteristics" given different regulatory capital requirements and risk profiles under NRB's Unified Directives.

Entity-wide disclosures (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="31-34" />) require revenue by product/service, by geographical area, and information about major customers (>10% of revenue). For BFIs, the geographical split across Nepal's provinces (Koshi, Madhesh, Bagmati, Gandaki, Lumbini, Karnali, Sudurpashchim) provides useful insight into lending concentration.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NFRS 8 applies only to **listed companies** — those whose shares or bonds are traded on NEPSE. Private companies and small businesses do not need to follow this standard.

**Key terms:**
- **Operating segment** — A part of the business that earns revenue, incurs expenses, and whose results are regularly reviewed by the top decision maker.
- **CODM (Chief Operating Decision Maker)** — The person or group who decides how to allocate resources across business segments. Usually the CEO or board.
- **Reportable segment** — A segment big enough to report separately — generally 10% or more of total revenue, profit, or assets.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies to entities with publicly traded equity or debt instruments, or those filing financial statements with a regulator for the purpose of issuing securities (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="2" />). In Nepal, this means all NEPSE-listed companies and entities filing with SEBON for public offerings.

- **Operating segment** — a component that engages in business activities, whose operating results are regularly reviewed by the CODM, and for which discrete financial information is available (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="5" />).
- **Quantitative thresholds** — An operating segment is reportable if it meets any one: (a) revenue ≥10% of combined revenue, (b) profit or loss ≥10% of the greater of combined profit or combined loss in absolute amount, or (c) assets ≥10% of combined assets (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="13" />).
- Reportable segments must represent at least 75% of external revenue; otherwise, additional segments must be identified until 75% is reached.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The scope exclusion for subsidiaries whose parent already provides segment information in consolidated accounts is important — but if a subsidiary is itself listed on NEPSE, it must provide its own segment disclosures regardless of the parent's reporting. The "all other segments" category must be disclosed separately from reconciling items, preventing entities from burying meaningful segments in reconciliation.

Measurement basis for segment information follows CODM reporting — if management measures segment profit before tax and excluding share of associates' profits, that is the reported measure. However, the entity must reconcile segment totals to the corresponding NFRS amounts (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="28" />).

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

NFRS 8 is a disclosure-only standard — it does not affect the recognition or measurement of items in the primary financial statements. Segment information is presented in the notes. However, the segment identification process can influence how management structures internal reporting and monitors performance, which indirectly affects resource allocation decisions.

## Measurement

Segment amounts are measured on the same basis as used in internal management reports reviewed by the CODM (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="25" />). This may differ from NFRS measurement — for example, if management evaluates segments using profit before depreciation, that measure is reported. The entity must explain the measurement basis and provide reconciliations to the amounts reported in the financial statements.

## Presentation & disclosure

Segment disclosures appear in the notes. For each reportable segment, the entity must disclose: a measure of profit or loss, total assets (if regularly provided to the CODM), total liabilities (if regularly provided), revenue from external customers, inter-segment revenue, interest revenue and expense, depreciation and amortisation, material items of income and expense, share of profit of associates and joint ventures, income tax expense, and material non-cash items (<ParaRef std="IFRS 8" para="23" />).

Entity-wide disclosures include: revenue from external customers by product/service, by geographical area (including Nepal's home country vs foreign), and information about major customers contributing 10% or more of total revenue.
