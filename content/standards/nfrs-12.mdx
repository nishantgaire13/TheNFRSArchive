## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

When a company has ownership stakes in other companies — subsidiaries, associates, or joint ventures — investors need to understand these relationships. NFRS 12 requires companies to disclose detailed information about these interests so that readers of financial statements can evaluate the nature and risks involved.

Think of it as transparency rules. If Nabil Bank owns an insurance company and a mutual fund company, NFRS 12 makes sure Nabil discloses enough information about each of these interests — what they are, what risks they carry, and how they affect the group's financial position.

The standard consolidates disclosure requirements that were previously scattered across multiple standards (NAS 27, NAS 28, NFRS 10, NFRS 11) into one comprehensive disclosure framework.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 12 is a disclosure-only standard that requires entities to provide information enabling users to evaluate: (a) the nature of, and risks associated with, interests in other entities, and (b) the effects of those interests on the entity's financial position, financial performance, and cash flows.

The standard covers disclosures for interests in subsidiaries (NFRS 10), joint arrangements (NFRS 11), associates (NAS 28), and unconsolidated structured entities. It applies to entities that have any of these interests, regardless of whether they are listed.

For Nepali groups, NFRS 12 is particularly relevant given the complex ownership structures common in family-controlled conglomerates and the BFI sector where banks hold stakes in insurance companies, finance companies, and fund management entities. NRB also prescribes additional disclosures beyond NFRS 12 for banking groups.

NFRS 12 is fully aligned with IFRS 12.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NFRS 12's objective-based approach (<ParaRef std="IFRS 12" para="1" />) means entities cannot hide behind technical compliance — the overarching goal is to provide information that helps users evaluate the nature and risks of interests and their financial effects. This principle-based framing allows auditors and regulators (SEBON, NRB) to challenge disclosures that are technically complete but substantively inadequate.

Significant judgments and assumptions (<ParaRef std="IFRS 12" para="7-9" />) must be disclosed — how the entity determined it has control/joint control/significant influence, particularly for borderline cases. In Nepal's concentrated ownership environment, entities must explain why a 25% holding constitutes significant influence when the remaining 75% is widely dispersed, or why a 45% holding does not constitute control despite board representation.

The unconsolidated structured entity disclosures (<ParaRef std="IFRS 12" para="24-31" />) have limited current application in Nepal but will grow as securitisation, project finance SPVs, and employee benefit trusts become more prevalent.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NFRS 12 applies to any entity that has interests in subsidiaries, joint arrangements, associates, or unconsolidated structured entities.

**Key terms:**
- **Interest in another entity** — Any contractual or non-contractual involvement that exposes the entity to variability of returns from the other entity's performance. This includes shareholding, debt, guarantees, and management contracts.
- **Structured entity** — An entity designed so that voting rights are not the dominant factor in deciding who controls it — often trusts, partnerships, or special purpose vehicles.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 12 applies to entities that have interests in subsidiaries, joint arrangements, associates, or unconsolidated structured entities (<ParaRef std="IFRS 12" para="2" />). The definition of "interest" is deliberately broad — it encompasses not only equity holdings but also debt instruments, liquidity support, guarantees, management fees, and any other involvement exposing the entity to variable returns.

Key disclosure categories include: significant judgments and assumptions (para 7-9), interests in subsidiaries (para 10-19), interests in joint arrangements and associates (para 20-23), and interests in unconsolidated structured entities (para 24-31).

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The scope interaction with NFRS 9 is important: financial instruments that are interests in other entities are measured under NFRS 9 (or NAS 28/NFRS 11) but disclosed under NFRS 12. Employee benefit plans (NAS 19) and post-employment benefit plans (NAS 26, if applicable) are excluded from NFRS 12's scope.

The "nature of interests" disclosures — name, principal place of business, proportion of ownership, and whether held directly or through subsidiaries — provide the foundation that NRB and SEBON use to assess group complexity, concentration risk, and related-party relationships in Nepali financial groups.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

NFRS 12 is purely a disclosure standard and does not affect the recognition or measurement of interests in other entities. Recognition is governed by NFRS 10 (subsidiaries), NFRS 11 (joint arrangements), NAS 28 (associates), and NFRS 9 (other financial interests). NFRS 12 operates alongside these standards to ensure adequate transparency.

## Measurement

No measurement provisions — NFRS 12 prescribes only disclosures. Measurement of the underlying interests follows the applicable recognition standard. However, certain NFRS 12 disclosures require summarised financial information for material subsidiaries and associates (revenue, profit/loss, total assets, total liabilities), which must be prepared on an NFRS-consistent basis.

## Presentation & disclosure

NFRS 12's disclosures include: (a) significant judgments and assumptions in determining control, joint control, or significant influence; (b) for subsidiaries — NCI summarised financial information, nature and extent of restrictions on transferring funds, consequences of changes in ownership; (c) for joint arrangements and associates — nature and extent of interests, summarised financial information, unrecognised share of losses; (d) for unconsolidated structured entities — nature, purpose, size, activities, how the entity is financed, and maximum exposure to loss.

For Nepali BFI groups, NRB additionally requires disclosure of group structure charts, intra-group exposures, and capital adequacy on a consolidated basis — supplementing NFRS 12's general requirements.
