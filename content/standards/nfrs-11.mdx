## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

Sometimes two or more companies decide to work together on a project instead of doing it alone. In Nepal, this is very common in hydropower — two companies might jointly build a power plant, sharing the costs and the electricity generated. NFRS 11 sets the rules for how to account for these **joint arrangements**.

There are two types of joint arrangements. A **joint operation** is where each partner has direct rights to specific assets and obligations for specific liabilities — like two companies jointly owning a hydropower plant where each takes their share of electricity. A **joint venture** is a separate company set up by the partners — like creating a new company called "ABC Hydropower Pvt Ltd" that both partners invest in.

The accounting is different for each type. In a joint operation, each partner records its own share of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses directly. In a joint venture, each partner uses the **equity method** — recording the investment as a single line item and adjusting it for their share of profits.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 11 establishes principles for financial reporting by entities that have interests in arrangements that are jointly controlled. The standard classifies all joint arrangements into two types based on the rights and obligations of the parties:

**Joint operations** — parties (joint operators) have rights to the assets and obligations for the liabilities of the arrangement. Each joint operator recognises its share of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses.

**Joint ventures** — parties (joint venturers) have rights to the net assets of the arrangement. Joint venturers apply the equity method under NAS 28.

The classification depends on the structure, legal form, contractual terms, and any other facts and circumstances — not on the label the parties use. In Nepal, hydropower joint arrangements are common: the Upper Karnali project, Arun III, and numerous smaller projects involve joint arrangements between Nepali and foreign developers. Construction joint ventures for road and bridge projects (often involving international contractors) are another significant application.

NFRS 11 is fully aligned with IFRS 11.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NFRS 11 replaced NAS 31 (Interests in Joint Ventures), eliminating the proportionate consolidation option for jointly controlled entities. The classification framework (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="14-19" />) requires assessment of: (a) the legal form of the separate vehicle (if any), (b) the contractual arrangement, and (c) other facts and circumstances — particularly whether the parties have rights to substantially all the economic output of the arrangement.

The "other facts and circumstances" assessment (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="B15-B33" />) is where judgment concentrates. A Nepali hydropower SPV structured as a private limited company (suggesting joint venture) may actually be a joint operation if the PPA requires each party to take its share of electricity output directly and the parties fund the entity's obligations in proportion to their interests. The substance of the arrangement, not its legal form, determines classification.

Nepal-India cross-border hydropower projects add complexity: government-to-government agreements, sovereign guarantees, and regulatory approvals from both Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) and India's Central Electricity Authority create layered contractual arrangements that require careful analysis under the NFRS 11 framework.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NFRS 11 applies when two or more companies share **joint control** over an arrangement. Joint control means all key decisions require unanimous consent of the partners.

**Key terms:**
- **Joint arrangement** — An arrangement where two or more parties have joint control.
- **Joint control** — Shared control where decisions about relevant activities require unanimous consent.
- **Joint operation** — Partners directly own their share of assets and owe their share of liabilities.
- **Joint venture** — A separate entity whose net assets belong to the partners jointly.
- **Equity method** — Recording the investment at cost plus share of post-acquisition profits, used for joint ventures.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies to all entities that are parties to a joint arrangement (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="3" />). A joint arrangement exists when: (a) the parties are bound by a contractual arrangement, and (b) the contractual arrangement gives two or more parties joint control (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="5" />).

- **Joint control** — Contractually agreed sharing of control, existing only when decisions about relevant activities require the unanimous consent of the parties sharing control (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="7" />).
- **Separate vehicle** — A separately identifiable financial structure (company, partnership, trust) through which the joint arrangement is conducted. Its existence does not automatically make the arrangement a joint venture.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The distinction between joint control and mere significant influence is critical. If a party has a veto right only over protective matters (capital structure changes, extraordinary transactions), it does not have joint control. Only rights over relevant activities — the activities that significantly affect the arrangement's returns — establish joint control (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="B5-B11" />).

An operator of a joint arrangement is not automatically a joint controller. Nepali hydropower arrangements often designate one party as "lead developer" with day-to-day management authority — but if the joint arrangement agreement requires unanimous consent for key decisions (capital expenditure above a threshold, PPA amendments, dividend declarations), joint control exists regardless of the management delegation.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

For **joint operations**, each joint operator recognises in its own financial statements: its assets (including its share of jointly held assets), its liabilities (including its share of jointly incurred liabilities), its revenue from the sale of its share of output, its share of revenue from joint sales, and its expenses (including its share of jointly incurred expenses) (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="20" />).

For **joint ventures**, each joint venturer recognises its interest as a single investment and accounts for it using the equity method in accordance with NAS 28 (<ParaRef std="IFRS 11" para="24" />).

## Measurement

Joint operation assets and liabilities are measured by the joint operator in accordance with the relevant NFRS applicable to those specific items (e.g., NAS 16 for PPE, NAS 2 for inventory). Joint venture investments under the equity method are initially recognised at cost and subsequently adjusted for the venturer's share of post-acquisition changes in net assets. Transactions between a joint operator/venturer and the arrangement require elimination of unrealised profits to the extent of the party's interest.

## Presentation & disclosure

Joint operation items are presented within the joint operator's own assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses — they are not shown as a single line item. Joint venture investments appear as a single line in the statement of financial position ("Investment in joint ventures") with profit share shown as a single line in profit or loss. Detailed disclosures about joint arrangements fall under NFRS 12, including the nature, extent, and financial effects of interests in joint arrangements.
