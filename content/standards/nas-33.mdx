## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

Earnings Per Share (EPS) tells you how much profit a company made for each share. If Nepal Telecom earned NPR 10 crore in profit and has 1 crore shares outstanding, each share "earned" NPR 10. This is a simple way for investors to compare companies of different sizes.

NAS 33 provides the formula for calculating EPS consistently so that all companies do it the same way. There are two types:
1. **Basic EPS** — profit divided by actual shares outstanding.
2. **Diluted EPS** — a "what if" calculation that assumes all convertible instruments (like convertible debentures) are converted into shares. This shows the worst-case scenario for existing shareholders.

**Why does this matter in Nepal?** NEPSE investors rely heavily on EPS to evaluate stocks. Bonus shares (stock dividends) are extremely popular in Nepal — when a company issues bonus shares, the number of shares increases but total profit stays the same, so EPS drops. Understanding NAS 33 helps investors interpret these changes correctly.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 33 prescribes the calculation and presentation of basic and diluted earnings per share. The standard applies to entities whose ordinary shares or potential ordinary shares are publicly traded, or that are in the process of listing — effectively all NEPSE-listed companies.

Basic EPS equals profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders divided by the weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding (WANOS) during the period. The weighting is time-proportional: shares issued on 1 Poush are weighted for only six months of a Shrawan-to-Ashad fiscal year. Bonus shares and share splits are treated as if they had been outstanding for the entire period (and all prior periods presented) because no new resources flow into the entity.

Diluted EPS adjusts both the numerator and denominator for the effects of all dilutive potential ordinary shares — convertible debentures, share options, contingently issuable shares. The key principle: only include potential shares if their inclusion reduces EPS (dilutive effect). Anti-dilutive instruments are excluded from the calculation.

In Nepal's market, bonus shares (stock dividends) are the dominant share issuance mechanism. Rights issues at below-market prices contain a bonus element that requires adjustment. SEBON mandates EPS disclosure in quarterly and annual filings, making NAS 33 compliance directly relevant for market reporting.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 33 implements a control number approach for dilution testing (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="42–43" />): continuing operations profit is the control number — if an instrument is anti-dilutive relative to continuing operations, it is excluded from diluted EPS even if it would be dilutive relative to total earnings. The sequencing rule (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="44" />) requires potential ordinary shares to be considered in order from most dilutive to least dilutive, stopping when overall EPS is no longer reduced.

The WANOS calculation requires careful treatment of Nepal-specific share events: (a) bonus shares under <ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="28" /> — retrospective adjustment to all periods as no consideration is received, (b) rights issues below fair value under <ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="26–27" /> — contain a bonus element requiring a theoretical ex-rights price (TERP) adjustment factor, (c) promoter shares released from lock-up are already included in WANOS as they were outstanding but restricted, not unissued.

The interaction with NAS 32's compound instrument classification is critical: convertible debentures classified as compound instruments under NAS 32 require diluted EPS adjustment — the numerator adds back the after-tax interest expense on the liability component, and the denominator adds the shares that would be issued on conversion. Nepal's BFI subordinated convertible debentures are prime candidates for this calculation. The "if-converted" method assumes conversion at the beginning of the period (or issue date, if later).

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 33 applies to companies whose shares are traded on NEPSE or that are filing to list on NEPSE. Private companies may also present EPS voluntarily.

**Key terms:**
- **Earnings** — The profit belonging to ordinary shareholders (after tax, after preference dividends).
- **Basic EPS** — Profit divided by the weighted average number of shares. The "actual" EPS.
- **Diluted EPS** — EPS assuming all convertible instruments are converted to shares. The "cautious" EPS.
- **Weighted average number of ordinary shares (WANOS)** — Shares outstanding, weighted by the time they were outstanding during the year.
- **Bonus shares** — Free additional shares given to existing shareholders (like a 1:1 bonus — one new share for each existing share). Very common in Nepal.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard is mandatory for entities with publicly traded ordinary shares or potential ordinary shares, and entities filing financial statements with a securities regulator for the purpose of issuing ordinary shares (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="2" />). In Nepal, this covers all NEPSE-listed entities and IPO applicants.

Key definitions:
- **Ordinary share** — an equity instrument subordinate to all other classes of equity instruments.
- **Potential ordinary share** — a financial instrument or contract that may entitle its holder to ordinary shares (convertible debentures, share options, warrants).
- **Dilution** — a reduction in EPS from assuming conversion or exercise of potential ordinary shares.
- **Anti-dilution** — an increase in EPS from assumed conversion — such instruments are excluded from diluted EPS.
- **Contingently issuable shares** — shares issuable for little or no consideration upon satisfaction of specified conditions.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

Scope nuances: (a) entities presenting both consolidated and separate financial statements must present EPS only in consolidated statements — separate-statement EPS is permitted but not required (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="4" />), (b) an entity disclosing earnings per share amounts in addition to basic and diluted EPS (e.g., "cash EPS") must calculate those using the WANOS denominator determined under NAS 33 and reconcile to reported profit (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="73" />). SEBON's reporting templates require both basic and diluted EPS in quarterly filings; many Nepali analysts also compute "annualised EPS" from quarterly data, which is not an NAS 33 measure but widely used in NEPSE valuation.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

NAS 33 is a presentation standard — it does not govern recognition of any transaction. EPS is computed from recognised profit or loss and presented on the face of the statement of profit or loss for each class of ordinary shares with differing rights (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="66" />). Both basic and diluted EPS must be presented with equal prominence. EPS must be presented for profit or loss from continuing operations and total profit or loss. If diluted EPS is reported for continuing operations, it must also be reported for discontinued operations (either on the face or in the notes).

## Measurement

**Basic EPS:** Numerator = profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent. Denominator = WANOS outstanding during the period, adjusted retrospectively for bonus issues and share splits (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="19–29" />). For bonus shares, WANOS is adjusted as if the bonus had been issued at the beginning of the earliest period presented. For rights issues at below market price, a theoretical ex-rights price (TERP) adjustment factor is applied.

**Diluted EPS:** The numerator is adjusted for the after-tax effects of dividends and interest on dilutive potential ordinary shares, and any other changes in income or expense that would result from conversion. The denominator is adjusted for the weighted average number of additional ordinary shares that would have been outstanding assuming conversion of all dilutive potential ordinary shares (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="33–40" />). The "if-converted" method applies to convertible instruments; the "treasury stock" method applies to options and warrants.

## Presentation & disclosure

Basic and diluted EPS are presented on the face of the statement of profit or loss for each class of ordinary shares (<ParaRef std="IAS 33" para="66–69" />). Disclosures include: the amounts used as numerators, reconciliation of those amounts to profit or loss, the WANOS used as denominators, instruments that could potentially dilute EPS in the future but were excluded as anti-dilutive, and a description of transactions after the reporting period that would have significantly changed the EPS denominators (such as bonus shares declared after year-end but before financial statements are authorised). In Nepal, SEBON additionally requires disclosure of the EPS trend for the past five years in annual reports of listed entities.
