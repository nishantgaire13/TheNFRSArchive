## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

A company's fiscal year in Nepal ends on 31 Ashad (mid-July). But the financial statements are not published immediately — it takes weeks or months to audit and finalise them. During this gap, things happen: a major customer might go bankrupt, an earthquake might damage a factory, or the board might declare a dividend.

NAS 10 answers one critical question: **should these later events change the numbers in the financial statements, or should they just be mentioned in the notes?**

The answer depends on whether the event provides evidence of a condition that **already existed** at the reporting date. If a customer was already in financial trouble at year-end and confirmed bankruptcy afterward, that is an **adjusting event** — the financial statements should be updated. But if a healthy factory was destroyed by a flood two weeks after year-end, that is a **non-adjusting event** — the financial statements keep their original numbers, but a note explains what happened. Think of it like a medical test: if blood was drawn on Ashad 31 and the results came back in Shrawan, the results reflect your health on Ashad 31, not Shrawan.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 10 defines events after the reporting period as those events, favourable and unfavourable, that occur between the end of the reporting period and the date the financial statements are authorised for issue. The standard distinguishes two categories:

**Adjusting events** provide evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period. Examples include: settlement of a court case confirming a present obligation, receipt of information indicating impairment of an asset (e.g., customer bankruptcy), discovery of fraud or errors, and determination of the cost of assets purchased or proceeds of assets sold before year-end.

**Non-adjusting events** indicate conditions that arose after the reporting period. Examples include: decline in market value of investments after year-end, natural disasters (earthquakes, floods), major business combinations or restructuring plans announced after the reporting date, and abnormally large changes in asset prices or foreign exchange rates.

The critical date is the **date of authorisation for issue** — in Nepal, this is typically the date the board of directors approves the financial statements, which for most entities falls 3-6 months after Ashad-end. NRB requires BFIs to submit audited financial statements within Kartik (approximately 4 months after fiscal year-end).

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 10 implements a condition-based test rather than a causation test. The question is not "what caused the event" but "did the condition exist at the reporting date" (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="3" />). This distinction matters in practice: a debtor's bankruptcy after year-end is adjusting because the financial distress existed at year-end — the bankruptcy merely confirms it. Conversely, a fire destroying inventory after year-end is non-adjusting because the inventory was intact at year-end.

The going concern assessment under <ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="14-16" /> is the most consequential aspect: if management determines after the reporting period that the entity will liquidate or cease trading, the financial statements shall not be prepared on a going concern basis. This creates a paradox — the event (decision to liquidate) occurred after the reporting date, yet NAS 10 treats it as requiring adjustment of the entire basis of preparation, not merely disclosure.

Nepal's seismic vulnerability makes the earthquake scenario a recurring exam and practice topic. The 2072 BS (2015) earthquakes occurred during the Nepali fiscal year for most entities, but for those with non-standard reporting periods, classification as adjusting or non-adjusting depended on the reporting date. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 similarly tested the standard: for entities with an Ashad 2076 (July 2019) year-end, COVID-19 was a non-adjusting event (conditions arose after year-end); for those with Ashad 2077 (July 2020) year-end, it was a condition existing at year-end.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope &amp; key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 10 covers events that happen between **the last day of the fiscal year** (Ashad-end) and **the date the financial statements are formally approved** by the board of directors.

**Key terms:**
- **Reporting period** — The fiscal year the financial statements cover (e.g., Shrawan 2081 to Ashad 2082).
- **Adjusting event** — An event that gives evidence about something that was already true at year-end. The numbers in the accounts should be updated.
- **Non-adjusting event** — An event that arose after year-end. The accounts keep the same numbers, but a note explains the event if it is important enough.
- **Date of authorisation for issue** — The date the board of directors (or equivalent body) approves the financial statements for release.
- **Dividends** — If dividends are declared after year-end, they are **not** recorded as a liability at year-end. They are a non-adjusting event and only disclosed in the notes.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies to all events occurring between the end of the reporting period and the date financial statements are authorised for issue (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="2" />). The authorisation date is a factual determination — in Nepal, it is typically the board approval date, even if the financial statements subsequently require shareholder approval at the Annual General Meeting (AGM).

Key technical concepts:
- **Adjusting events** (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="8-9" />) — Require adjustment of amounts recognised in the financial statements. Include: resolution of court cases, bankruptcy of customers, sale of inventory at NRV below cost (evidence of NRV at year-end), discovery of fraud.
- **Non-adjusting events** (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="10-11" />) — Require disclosure of nature and estimated financial effect if material. Include: major acquisitions, decline in fair values, natural disasters.
- **Dividends** (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="12-13" />) — Dividends declared after the reporting period are not recognised as a liability at the reporting date. Disclosed as a non-adjusting event. This catches many Nepali entities off-guard, as the pre-NFRS practice was to accrue proposed dividends.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The authorisation date determination under <ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="4-6" /> requires understanding the entity's governance structure. In Nepal, most private companies have a single-tier board whose approval is the authorisation date. For public companies, the board approves financial statements which are then placed before the AGM — NAS 10 requires the board approval date as the cut-off, even if shareholders can modify the financial statements at the AGM (which is rare in practice but legally possible under Nepal's Companies Act 2063).

Scope interaction with NAS 37 (Provisions) is critical: if a present obligation existed at the reporting date but was uncertain, and post-year-end evidence confirms the obligation and its amount, NAS 37's recognition criteria were met at year-end — NAS 10 merely confirms this. Conversely, if the obligating event occurs after year-end, NAS 37 does not require a provision, and NAS 10 treats it as non-adjusting. The boundary is tested with restructuring plans: a plan announced internally before year-end but announced to affected parties only after year-end typically fails NAS 37's constructive obligation test at year-end.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

Adjusting events are recognised by updating the amounts in the financial statements. If an adjusting event reveals that the going concern assumption is no longer appropriate, the entire basis of preparation changes — the entity must not prepare its financial statements on a going concern basis (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="14" />).

Non-adjusting events are not recognised in the financial statements. However, if a non-adjusting event is material, the entity discloses the nature of the event and an estimate of its financial effect (or a statement that such an estimate cannot be made) in the notes (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="21" />).

Dividends declared after the reporting period are specifically addressed: they are not recognised as a liability at the reporting date because no obligation exists at that date (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="12" />). This applies to both cash dividends and stock dividends. In Nepal, BFIs typically declare dividends after NRB approval of their financial statements, making this provision particularly relevant.

## Measurement

Adjusting events may affect the measurement of assets and liabilities at the reporting date. For example, if a debtor goes bankrupt after year-end (confirming financial difficulty at year-end), the receivable's expected credit loss under NFRS 9 must be updated. If inventory sold after year-end at below cost confirms NRV was lower than cost at year-end, the inventory write-down under NAS 2 must be adjusted. The measurement adjustment reflects the condition as it existed at the reporting date, using post-year-end evidence to improve the estimate.

## Presentation &amp; disclosure

The entity must disclose the date the financial statements were authorised for issue and who gave that authorisation (<ParaRef std="IAS 10" para="17" />). For material non-adjusting events, the entity discloses the nature of the event and an estimate of its financial effect, or a statement that such an estimate cannot be made. In Nepal, common non-adjusting event disclosures include post-year-end NRB directives affecting BFI operations, major changes in NRB policy rates, SEBON regulatory actions, and natural disaster impacts.

If an entity receives information after the reporting period about conditions that existed at the reporting date, it must update disclosures relating to those conditions in light of the new information. This is separate from adjusting recognised amounts — even non-recognition disclosures (such as contingent liabilities under NAS 37) must be updated if post-year-end information is relevant to the condition at year-end.
