## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

Every profitable business pays income tax. That is "current tax" — straightforward. But NAS 12 also requires businesses to account for "deferred tax," which is trickier.

**What is deferred tax?** Sometimes, the tax rules and accounting rules give different answers. For example:
- Accounting says depreciate a machine over 10 years (NPR 1,00,000/year)
- Tax law says depreciate at 20% declining balance (NPR 2,00,000 in year 1)

This means you pay less tax now (good!) but more tax later (because you used up the tax depreciation faster). NAS 12 requires you to record this future tax as a "deferred tax liability" — tax you will owe later.

The reverse can also happen: you might pay more tax now and less later, creating a "deferred tax asset."

**Simple rule:** Deferred tax tracks the tax consequences of differences between your accounting books and your tax return.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 12 deals with how entities account for income taxes — both the current tax payable on this year's profits and the deferred tax arising from temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities in the financial statements and their tax bases.

Deferred tax accounting is one of the most technically demanding areas for Nepali preparers. Nepal's Income Tax Act 2058 creates numerous differences from NAS/NFRS carrying amounts — different depreciation rates, different recognition timing for provisions, and specific tax exemptions for certain industries. Each of these differences generates a deferred tax asset or liability that must be measured and disclosed.

NAS 12 is fully aligned with IAS 12.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 12 implements a comprehensive balance-sheet-based approach to deferred tax, comparing the carrying amount of each asset and liability with its tax base to identify temporary differences. This replaced the older income-statement-based "timing difference" approach and captures items that bypass P&L entirely (revaluations, remeasurements recognised in OCI, business combination fair value adjustments).

The standard's interaction with Nepal's multi-rate tax environment is analytically complex: entities must apply the rate expected at reversal, not the current rate. This creates a look-through requirement where each temporary difference is mapped to a future reversal profile and matched with the applicable rate (25% standard, 30% BFI, or concessional rates for SEZ/priority sectors). Rate changes enacted but not yet effective require full remeasurement of deferred tax balances — a non-trivial exercise when Nepal's Finance Acts periodically adjust sector-specific rates.

The initial recognition exemption (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="15" />) prevents grossing up of assets and liabilities in non-business-combination transactions, but creates a permanent unrecognised temporary difference that requires ongoing tracking — a compliance burden that many Nepali entities manage poorly.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 12 applies to all income taxes — whether paid in Nepal or abroad.

**Key terms:**
- **Current tax** — The tax you actually pay this year based on taxable profit.
- **Deferred tax** — Tax that will come due in the future because of timing differences between accounting and tax rules.
- **Temporary difference** — When the book value of an asset/liability differs from its tax value. These differences are temporary because they eventually reverse.
- **Tax base** — What the tax authority considers the value of an asset or liability for tax calculation purposes.

**Two types of temporary differences:**
- **Taxable** → You will pay MORE tax in the future → Creates a deferred tax LIABILITY
- **Deductible** → You will pay LESS tax in the future → Creates a deferred tax ASSET

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies to all income taxes, domestic and foreign (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="1" />).

Key terms:
- **Current tax** — income tax payable/recoverable for the current period.
- **Deferred tax liabilities** — taxes payable in future periods for taxable temporary differences.
- **Deferred tax assets** — taxes recoverable in future periods for deductible temporary differences, carried-forward losses, and unused tax credits.
- **Temporary differences** — differences between carrying amount and tax base, either taxable (→ DTL) or deductible (→ DTA).
- **Tax base** — the amount attributed to an asset or liability for tax purposes.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

Tax base determination requires analysis of the specific tax treatment of each item under Nepal's Income Tax Act 2058. For assets, the tax base is the amount deductible for tax purposes against future taxable economic benefits — if benefits are not taxable, the tax base equals the carrying amount (no temporary difference). For liabilities, the tax base is the carrying amount minus amounts deductible for tax when the liability is settled.

Key boundary cases: (a) provisions where tax deductibility requires cash payment (warranty, leave encashment) — tax base is zero, creating deductible temporary differences; (b) goodwill with no tax base (not deductible in Nepal) — taxable temporary difference but exempt from recognition under the initial recognition exemption; (c) undistributed profits of subsidiaries — generally a taxable temporary difference unless the parent controls the timing of reversal and it is probable that the difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="39" />).

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

**Current tax:** liability for amount unpaid, or asset if overpaid (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="12" />).

**Deferred tax liabilities:** recognised for all taxable temporary differences, with exceptions for initial recognition of goodwill and certain non-business-combination transactions (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="15" />).

**Deferred tax assets:** recognised only to the extent probable that future taxable profit will be available (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="24" />). Nepal allows 7-year loss carry-forward (5 years for banking/insurance).

## Measurement

Measured at rates expected to apply when the difference reverses — using enacted or substantively enacted rates (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="47" />). Nepal rates: 25% standard, 30% BFIs, 20% SEZ entities. Deferred tax is not discounted.

## Presentation & disclosure

Current and deferred tax presented separately. Offsetting permitted within same tax jurisdiction with legal right to set off. Disclosures: major components of tax expense, rate reconciliation, deferred tax by type, unrecognised DTAs (<ParaRef std="IAS 12" para="79" />).
