## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

How much is something worth? NFRS 13 provides a single answer: **fair value** is the price you would receive if you sold an asset, or the price you would pay to transfer a liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants.

Think of it as the market price. If you want to know the fair value of 100 shares of Nepal Telecom, you look at the NEPSE closing price. That is a **Level 1** input — a quoted price in an active market. But what about things that are not traded on a stock exchange, like a piece of land in Pokhara or an unquoted investment? For those, you need to use other methods — comparable sales (Level 2) or calculations based on estimates (Level 3).

NFRS 13 does not decide **when** to use fair value — other standards like NFRS 9, NAS 16, and NAS 40 determine that. NFRS 13 only provides the **how** — the measurement framework and required disclosures.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 13 defines fair value, establishes a framework for measuring it, and requires disclosures about fair value measurements. The standard applies whenever another NFRS requires or permits fair value measurement or disclosures — it is a cross-cutting measurement standard, not one that mandates fair value for any specific item.

Fair value is defined as the **exit price** — the price received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="9" />). It is a market-based measurement, not entity-specific.

The **fair value hierarchy** prioritises inputs:
- **Level 1** — Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets/liabilities (e.g., NEPSE share prices).
- **Level 2** — Observable inputs other than Level 1 quotes (e.g., interest rates from NRB, comparable property transactions).
- **Level 3** — Unobservable inputs based on the entity's own assumptions (e.g., DCF models for unquoted investments).

In Nepal, Level 1 inputs are available for NEPSE-listed securities. Most other fair value measurements rely on Level 2 or Level 3 inputs, creating significant estimation challenges.

NFRS 13 is fully aligned with IFRS 13.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NFRS 13 unifies a previously fragmented fair value measurement framework. The exit price notion (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="24" />) assumes the transaction takes place in the principal market (greatest volume and activity) or, in its absence, the most advantageous market (maximises amount received/minimises amount paid, after transaction and transport costs). For Nepal, identifying the principal market is challenging: NEPSE is the only equity exchange, NRB interbank rates define the money market, but no active market exists for corporate bonds, derivatives, or most property types.

The highest and best use concept for non-financial assets (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="27-33" />) is particularly relevant for Nepali land valuations. Agricultural land in the Kathmandu Valley may have highest and best use as commercial/residential development — the fair value should reflect this alternative use if physically possible, legally permissible, and financially feasible, even if the current owner uses it for farming.

Valuation techniques — the market approach, income approach, and cost approach (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="62" />) — must be applied consistently. BFI financial instruments often require the income approach (DCF) using discount rates derived from NRB treasury bill auctions and interbank rates. The credit valuation adjustment (CVA) and debit valuation adjustment (DVA) for derivative fair values are conceptually required but rarely applied in Nepal given the minimal derivatives market.

Level 3 fair value measurements require extensive disclosures: valuation techniques, inputs used, reconciliation of opening to closing balances, sensitivity analysis, and the effect of changing unobservable inputs (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="93" />).

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NFRS 13 applies whenever another standard requires or allows fair value measurement. It does **not** apply to share-based payment (NFRS 2), leases (NFRS 16), NRV of inventories (NAS 2), or value in use for impairment (NAS 36).

**Key terms:**
- **Fair value** — The price to sell an asset or transfer a liability between willing, knowledgeable parties.
- **Active market** — A market where transactions happen frequently enough to provide continuous pricing information (like NEPSE for listed shares).
- **Market participants** — Buyers and sellers who are independent, knowledgeable, able, and willing to transact.
- **Observable inputs** — Information available from market data (prices, rates, indices).
- **Unobservable inputs** — The entity's own assumptions about what market participants would use.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 13 applies when another NFRS requires or permits fair value measurements or disclosures about fair value (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="5" />). Key scope exclusions: NFRS 2 (share-based payment), NFRS 16 (leases), NAS 2 (NRV, which is entity-specific), NAS 36 (value in use, also entity-specific), and NAS 19 (plan assets at fair value — fair value is the same but certain disclosures differ).

Key concepts:
- **Unit of account** — The level at which an asset or liability is aggregated for recognition purposes under the applicable NFRS (e.g., individual share, portfolio, CGU).
- **Transaction costs** — Costs to sell are not a characteristic of the asset — excluded from fair value but included when determining the most advantageous market.
- **Transport costs** — Costs to transport an asset to its principal market are deducted from the selling price when measuring fair value.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NFRS 13's scope is carefully delineated to avoid conflict with entity-specific measurements. NRV under NAS 2 and value in use under NAS 36 are explicitly excluded because they reflect entity-specific assumptions, whereas fair value is market-based. However, fair value less costs of disposal under NAS 36 does fall within NFRS 13's scope — creating an asymmetry where one leg of the recoverable amount test uses NFRS 13 and the other does not.

The portfolio exception (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="48" />) allows fair value measurement at the portfolio level for groups of financial assets and liabilities with offsetting market or credit risk positions — relevant for BFI treasury portfolios. Day-one gains and losses (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="57-59" />) are recognised only when fair value is evidenced by Level 1 or Level 2 inputs; Level 3-only measurements defer the gain on initial recognition.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

NFRS 13 does not establish recognition criteria — it is purely a measurement and disclosure framework. Whether an item is measured at fair value is determined by the applicable standard (e.g., NFRS 9 for financial instruments at FVTPL or FVOCI, NAS 16 for the revaluation model, NAS 40 for investment property under the fair value model). NFRS 13 then provides the methodology for that measurement.

## Measurement

The fair value measurement process involves: (1) identifying the asset or liability and its unit of account, (2) determining the principal or most advantageous market, (3) selecting the appropriate valuation technique(s) — market approach, income approach, or cost approach, (4) categorising inputs into the three-level hierarchy, and (5) applying the technique consistently using market participant assumptions.

For non-financial assets, fair value reflects the highest and best use. For liabilities and the entity's own equity instruments, fair value assumes transfer (not settlement) and includes the effect of non-performance risk. Financial liabilities with a demand feature have a floor at the amount payable on demand, discounted from the earliest date of demand.

## Presentation & disclosure

NFRS 13 requires disclosure of fair value measurements by hierarchy level. For recurring fair value measurements (measured each reporting period), entities must disclose: the fair value at the end of the reporting period, the level of the hierarchy, valuation techniques and inputs, and for Level 3 measurements — a reconciliation from opening to closing balances, total gains/losses included in P&L and OCI, and quantitative sensitivity analysis to reasonably possible changes in unobservable inputs (<ParaRef std="IFRS 13" para="91-99" />).

For non-recurring measurements (e.g., assets held for sale under NFRS 5), similar but reduced disclosures are required. Transfers between levels must be disclosed with reasons.
