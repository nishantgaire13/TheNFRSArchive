## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

When a business buys a building, a machine, or a vehicle, these are called "Property, Plant and Equipment" (PPE). Unlike office supplies that get used up quickly, PPE lasts for years.

NAS 16 answers three key questions:
1. **What cost do you record?** Not just the purchase price — include delivery, installation, and setup costs too.
2. **How do you spread the cost?** Since a machine lasts many years, you spread its cost over those years through "depreciation." A machine costing NPR 10,00,000 with a 10-year life costs NPR 1,00,000 per year in depreciation.
3. **Can you update the value?** Yes, using the "revaluation model" — but if you revalue one building, you must revalue all buildings.

**Why does this matter?** PPE is often the biggest item on a company's balance sheet. Getting its value right directly affects reported profits.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 16 is the foundation standard for tangible long-lived assets — land, buildings, machinery, vehicles, furniture, and equipment. It governs when to capitalise an expenditure, how to measure the asset initially and subsequently, and how to depreciate it over its useful life.

For Nepali entities, PPE often represents the single largest item on the balance sheet. Manufacturing companies in the Terai industrial corridor, hydropower developers, and hotel operators in the tourism sector all have significant PPE balances. Getting the accounting right directly affects reported profit (through depreciation charges), asset values (revaluation or cost model), and impairment assessments.

NAS 16 is fully aligned with IAS 16 — the rules are identical.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 16 operationalises the Conceptual Framework's asset definition for tangible items: an entity controls a resource from which future economic benefits are expected to flow. The recognition threshold is probability-weighted — "probable" future benefits, not certain — which in practice means virtually all acquired PPE is capitalised.

The standard's component accounting requirement (<ParaRef std="IAS 16" para="43" />) is architecturally significant for Nepal's infrastructure sector. Hydropower plants must disaggregate civil structures (40–50 year life), electromechanical equipment (20–25 years), and transmission infrastructure (15–20 years). Banks revaluing premises must separate land (no depreciation) from building structure, fit-out, and plant.

The revaluation model introduces OCI complexity: upward revaluations create revaluation surplus in equity, subsequent downward revaluations first reduce surplus then hit P&L. The surplus is transferred to retained earnings as the asset is consumed — an "excess depreciation" transfer that many Nepali entities omit. NRB mandates periodic revaluation for bank-owned properties, creating a sector-specific policy constraint.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 16 covers tangible (physical) assets that a business uses for more than one year:
- Land and buildings
- Machinery and equipment
- Vehicles
- Furniture and fixtures
- Computers and office equipment

**Not covered:** Inventory (NAS 2), leased assets (NFRS 16), or intangible items like software (NAS 38).

**Key terms:**
- **Depreciation** — Spreading the cost of an asset over its useful life. Like dividing a big expense into smaller annual portions.
- **Useful life** — How long the business expects to use the asset.
- **Residual value** — What the asset will be worth when the business is done using it (like the trade-in value of a car).

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies to property, plant and equipment except where another standard requires different treatment — biological assets (NAS 41), mineral rights (NFRS 6), and right-of-use assets (NFRS 16).

Key terms:
- **Cost** — the amount paid plus directly attributable costs to bring the asset to working condition.
- **Depreciation** — systematic allocation of the depreciable amount over useful life.
- **Residual value** — estimated disposal amount at end of useful life.
- **Carrying amount** — cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

Scope boundary cases in Nepal: (a) bearer plants (tea, coffee) — within NAS 16 since 2016 amendments (produce remains in NAS 41), (b) investment property held for rental yield — NAS 40 applies unless the entity elects cost model (in which case NAS 16 depreciation/disclosure requirements apply), (c) assets held for sale — NFRS 5 supersedes NAS 16 when classification criteria are met. The standard's control-based recognition test means assets under finance leases were historically within NAS 16's disclosure scope; NFRS 16's right-of-use model now governs these separately.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

An item of PPE is recognised when it is probable that future economic benefits will flow to the entity and the cost can be measured reliably (<ParaRef std="IAS 16" para="7" />). Component accounting requires significant parts with different useful lives to be depreciated separately (<ParaRef std="IAS 16" para="43" />).

## Measurement

**Initial measurement:** at cost, including purchase price, import duties, directly attributable costs, and qualifying borrowing costs under NAS 23 (<ParaRef std="IAS 16" para="16" />).

**Subsequent measurement — two models:**
1. **Cost model** — cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment. Most commonly used by Nepali entities.
2. **Revaluation model** — revalued amount less subsequent depreciation and impairment. Must revalue entire class when used (<ParaRef std="IAS 16" para="31" />).

**Depreciation:** straight-line (most common), reducing balance, or units of production. Useful lives and residual values reviewed annually (<ParaRef std="IAS 16" para="51" />).

## Presentation & disclosure

PPE presented by class with reconciliation of carrying amounts. Disclose measurement basis, methods, useful lives, and gross amounts (<ParaRef std="IAS 16" para="73" />).
