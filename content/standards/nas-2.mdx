## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

Inventory means the goods a business has available for sale or the materials it uses to make products. A grocery store's inventory is the food on its shelves. A factory's inventory includes raw materials, half-finished products, and finished goods ready to ship.

NAS 2 answers one big question: **what value do you put on inventory in your accounts?**

The rule is simple: record inventory at whichever is lower — what it cost you, or what you can sell it for (after deducting selling costs). This is called the "lower of cost and net realisable value" rule.

**Why does this matter?** If a store bought 100 phones at NPR 20,000 each but the market price dropped to NPR 15,000, the store cannot pretend they are still worth NPR 20,000. It must write them down to NPR 15,000.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 2 prescribes the accounting treatment for inventories — the goods an entity holds for sale in the ordinary course of business, in the process of production, or in the form of materials and supplies to be consumed in production. The core principle: inventories are measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV).

This standard matters deeply for Nepali manufacturing, trading, and retail entities. Industries like garment manufacturing, pharmaceutical production, food processing, and hardware trading all carry significant inventory balances. Misstating inventory directly misstates both profit and total assets.

NAS 2 is fully aligned with IAS 2.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 2 implements a historical-cost-with-ceiling model: cost is the primary measurement attribute, but NRV provides an upper bound reflecting the Conceptual Framework's prudence/faithful representation requirements. NRV is entity-specific (not fair value) — it considers the entity's particular selling circumstances, including contractual commitments.

The standard's cost flow assumptions (FIFO and weighted average) are proxies for physical flow tracking. LIFO's prohibition reflects the IASB's view that it systematically understates balance-sheet values in inflationary environments — particularly relevant in Nepal where CPI inflation has averaged 6–8% over the past decade. The overhead allocation methodology for manufactured inventory (<ParaRef std="IAS 2" para="12" />) requires normal-capacity-based fixed overhead absorption — a concept many Nepali manufacturers struggle with when actual production fluctuates seasonally.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 2 applies to all goods a business holds for sale or uses in production. It does NOT apply to: construction projects in progress (NFRS 15), financial investments (NFRS 9), or farm animals and crops (NAS 41).

**Key terms:**
- **Cost** — What you paid to buy or make the inventory, including purchase price, shipping, and customs duty.
- **Net Realisable Value (NRV)** — What you can sell the inventory for, minus any costs to complete or sell it.
- **FIFO** — First In, First Out. The oldest inventory is sold first. Like items at the front of a shelf being sold before items at the back.
- **Weighted average** — Calculate one average cost for all similar items.

**Important:** LIFO (Last In, First Out) is NOT allowed under NAS 2.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies to all inventories except work in progress under construction contracts (NFRS 15), financial instruments (NFRS 9), and biological assets (NAS 41) (<ParaRef std="IAS 2" para="2" />).

Key terms:
- **Net realisable value (NRV)** — estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and costs to sell. NRV is entity-specific; fair value is not.
- **Cost formulas** — FIFO or weighted average. LIFO is prohibited (<ParaRef std="IAS 2" para="25" />).

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The NRV assessment's unit of analysis is critical: generally item-by-item, but grouping is permitted for items within the same product line sharing similar purposes, produced and marketed in the same geographical area, and impracticable to evaluate individually. Raw materials held for use in production are not written down below cost if the finished goods they will be incorporated into are expected to sell at or above cost (<ParaRef std="IAS 2" para="32" />) — a frequently tested exception.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

Inventories are recognised as assets when the entity has control and future economic benefits are probable. They are derecognised (expensed as cost of goods sold) when the related revenue is recognised (<ParaRef std="IAS 2" para="34" />). NRV write-downs are expensed in the period they occur; reversals (up to original cost) reduce cost of goods sold.

## Measurement

**Cost includes:** purchase price, import duties, transport and handling, and conversion costs (direct labour plus systematic allocation of fixed and variable production overheads based on normal capacity). **Excluded:** abnormal waste, storage costs, administrative overheads, selling costs (<ParaRef std="IAS 2" para="16" />).

**Cost formulas:** specific identification for non-interchangeable items; FIFO or weighted average for interchangeable items — applied consistently within each class.

## Presentation & disclosure

Inventories presented as current assets, classified by nature. Disclose: accounting policies, carrying amounts by classification, cost of inventories expensed, write-downs and reversals (<ParaRef std="IAS 2" para="36" />).
