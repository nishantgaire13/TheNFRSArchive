## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

When a company owns other companies (called subsidiaries), it needs to show the big picture — the combined financial position of the whole group. Think of it like a family budget: instead of looking at each family member's finances separately, you combine everything to see the family's total income, total expenses, and total savings.

NFRS 10 requires parent companies to prepare **consolidated financial statements** — a single set of accounts that combines the parent and all its subsidiaries as if they were one entity. If Nabil Bank owns an insurance company and a fund management company, the consolidated statements show the combined assets, liabilities, income, and expenses of the entire Nabil group.

The key question NFRS 10 answers is: **does the parent control the subsidiary?** Control means the parent has power over the subsidiary, receives variable returns from it, and can use its power to affect those returns.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NFRS 10 establishes principles for presenting consolidated financial statements when an entity controls one or more other entities. The standard introduced a single control model replacing the previous dual model under NAS 27 (old version) and SIC-12.

**Control** exists when the investor has: (a) power over the investee — existing rights that give current ability to direct relevant activities, (b) exposure or rights to variable returns from involvement, and (c) ability to use power to affect returns (<ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="7" />).

Consolidation is relatively new in Nepal. Most Nepali corporate groups historically prepared only separate financial statements. NRB now requires BFI parent companies to prepare consolidated statements, and SEBON requires listed parent companies to file consolidated accounts. Many groups are still building consolidation capabilities — group-level accounting systems, intercompany reconciliation processes, and uniform accounting policies across subsidiaries.

NFRS 10 is fully aligned with IFRS 10.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NFRS 10's control model (<ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="5-18" />) requires continuous reassessment when facts and circumstances change. In Nepal's banking sector, this assessment is complicated by NRB's regulatory requirements: a bank may hold >50% of a subsidiary's shares but face NRB restrictions on exercising certain rights, raising the question of whether regulatory constraints negate "power." The distinction between protective rights and substantive rights (<ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="B26-B28" />) is critical here.

De facto control — control without a majority of voting rights — is relevant for Nepali family-owned conglomerates where a 30-40% shareholding combined with wide dispersion of remaining shares, board representation, and contractual arrangements may constitute control. The assessment of potential voting rights (<ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="B47-B50" />) adds further complexity when convertible instruments or warrants exist.

The investment entity exception (<ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="27" />) — measuring investments at fair value through profit or loss instead of consolidating — has limited application in Nepal but may become relevant as venture capital and private equity structures develop.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NFRS 10 applies to any entity that is a **parent** — meaning it controls one or more other entities (subsidiaries). A few exceptions exist: a parent need not consolidate if it is itself a wholly-owned subsidiary and certain conditions are met.

**Key terms:**
- **Parent** — A company that controls another company.
- **Subsidiary** — A company controlled by a parent.
- **Control** — Having power over the investee, being exposed to variable returns, and being able to use power to affect those returns.
- **Non-controlling interest (NCI)** — The portion of a subsidiary owned by outside shareholders (not the parent).
- **Consolidation** — Combining the financial statements of parent and subsidiaries line by line, eliminating intercompany transactions.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies to all parent entities except those qualifying for the exemption in <ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="4" />: the parent is itself a wholly or partially owned subsidiary, its owners do not object, it does not file with a securities regulator, and its ultimate or intermediate parent produces NFRS-compliant consolidated statements.

Key concepts:
- **Relevant activities** — Activities that significantly affect the investee's returns (e.g., lending decisions for a bank, tariff negotiations for a hydropower company).
- **Power** — Existing substantive rights (voting rights, board seats, contractual rights) to direct relevant activities.
- **Structured entities** — Entities where voting rights are not the dominant factor in determining control (e.g., SPVs, trusts). Uncommon in Nepal but emerging in securitisation structures.
- **Uniform accounting policies** — The parent must apply uniform accounting policies across all group entities for consolidation purposes.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

Scope interactions are significant: NFRS 10 determines the consolidation boundary, NFRS 3 governs initial measurement on acquisition, NFRS 12 prescribes disclosures about the nature and risks of interests, and NAS 27 deals with separate financial statements. The consolidation process itself requires: uniform accounting policies, common reporting dates (or adjustment within three months), elimination of intercompany balances and transactions, and allocation of comprehensive income between parent and NCI.

Changes in ownership without loss of control are equity transactions (<ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="23" />) — no gain/loss in profit or loss, no goodwill remeasurement. Loss of control triggers derecognition of subsidiary assets/liabilities at carrying amount, recognition of any retained investment at fair value, and recognition of resulting gain/loss in profit or loss (<ParaRef std="IFRS 10" para="25" />).

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

On the date a parent acquires control of a subsidiary, it begins consolidation. All identifiable assets and liabilities of the subsidiary are recognised at fair value under NFRS 3 (acquisition method). Non-controlling interests are measured either at fair value (full goodwill method) or at the NCI's proportionate share of the subsidiary's net identifiable assets. Goodwill is recognised as the excess of consideration transferred plus NCI plus any previously held equity interest over net identifiable assets.

When control is lost, the subsidiary is deconsolidated from that date. Any retained interest is remeasured to fair value, and the gain or loss is recognised in profit or loss.

## Measurement

After acquisition, consolidated financial statements are prepared by combining like items of assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses, and cash flows of the parent and subsidiary. Intercompany transactions, balances, income, and expenses are eliminated in full. Profit or loss and each component of OCI are attributed to owners of the parent and to NCI. Total comprehensive income is attributed even if NCI results in a deficit balance.

## Presentation & disclosure

Consolidated financial statements present the group as a single economic entity. NCI is presented in the consolidated statement of financial position within equity, separately from parent equity. NCI's share of profit or loss and OCI is shown on the face of the respective statements. Detailed disclosure requirements fall under NFRS 12, including the nature and extent of significant restrictions, the nature of risks associated with interests in consolidated and unconsolidated entities, and the consequences of changes in ownership.
