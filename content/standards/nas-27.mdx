## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

When a large company (the "parent") owns smaller companies (its "subsidiaries"), it usually needs to prepare combined accounts showing the entire group as one — these are called consolidated financial statements. But the parent can also prepare its own standalone accounts, showing investments in subsidiaries as line items rather than combining everything together. These standalone accounts are called "separate financial statements."

For example, Nepal Telecom might own subsidiary companies in different business lines. In its separate financial statements, Nepal Telecom shows its investment in each subsidiary as a single number (say NPR 50 crore) rather than combining all the subsidiary's individual assets, liabilities, and revenues line by line.

**Why does this matter?** In Nepal, most company groups have historically only prepared separate financial statements — consolidated accounts are relatively new. Understanding the difference helps investors know whether they are seeing the full group picture or just one entity.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 27 prescribes the accounting and disclosure requirements for investments in subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates when an entity prepares separate financial statements. "Separate financial statements" are those presented by a parent (or an investor with joint control or significant influence) in which the investments are accounted for at cost, in accordance with NFRS 9, or using the equity method — rather than on the basis of the investee's reported results and net assets (as in consolidation).

In Nepal, NAS 27 has particular relevance because consolidated financial statements are a relatively recent requirement. Prior to NFRS adoption in 2018, most Nepali groups only filed separate entity statements. NRB now requires BFI parents to prepare consolidated financial statements under NFRS 10, but many non-financial groups continue to primarily use separate financial statements. NAS 27 ensures that even in these standalone statements, investments in group entities are properly accounted for and disclosed.

NAS 27 is fully aligned with IAS 27 — no carve-outs exist.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 27 (revised 2011) was narrowed in scope when NFRS 10 took over consolidated financial statement requirements. The standard now deals exclusively with separate financial statements — the accounting by a parent, venture partner, or investor for investments in subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates when it elects (or is required by local regulation) to present separate financial statements.

The three measurement options — cost, NFRS 9 (fair value through profit or loss or fair value through OCI), and equity method (<ParaRef std="IAS 27" para="10" />) — create significant policy choice consequences. In Nepal, most entities use the cost method for simplicity, but this means dividend income is recognised only when declared (not as the investee earns profits), creating potential for earnings management through dividend timing. The equity method option, added by the 2014 amendment, allows the separate financial statements to reflect the investor's share of the investee's results — but few Nepali entities have adopted this.

The interaction with NFRS 5 (Non-current Assets Held for Sale) means investments classified as held for sale are measured under NFRS 5, not NAS 27. The standard's requirement for consistent policy within each category of investment (<ParaRef std="IAS 27" para="11" />) — subsidiaries as one category, associates as another, joint ventures as another — constrains cherry-picking of measurement methods.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 27 applies when a parent company prepares its own individual accounts (not the combined group accounts). It covers how to show:
- **Investments in subsidiaries** — companies you control
- **Investments in associates** — companies you significantly influence (usually 20–50% ownership)
- **Investments in joint ventures** — businesses you jointly control with others

**Key terms:**
- **Separate financial statements** — the accounts of a single entity where investments in subsidiaries are shown as a single line item, not combined line by line.
- **Consolidated financial statements** — the group accounts that combine everything together (covered by NFRS 10, not NAS 27).
- **Cost method** — recording the investment at what you paid for it. Most common in Nepal.
- **Equity method** — adjusting the investment balance for your share of the investee's profits and losses each year.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard applies when an entity prepares separate financial statements that are either its only financial statements or are presented in addition to consolidated financial statements (NFRS 10), jointly controlled entity statements (NFRS 11), or associate/joint venture statements (NAS 28).

Key definitions:
- **Separate financial statements** — those presented by a parent or an investor in which investments are accounted for at cost, under NFRS 9, or using the equity method (<ParaRef std="IAS 27" para="4" />).
- **Parent** — an entity that controls one or more entities.
- **Subsidiary** — an entity controlled by another entity. Control exists when the investor is exposed to variable returns and has the ability to affect those returns through power over the investee (NFRS 10 definition).
- **Measurement policy choice** — the entity must apply the same accounting policy (cost, NFRS 9, or equity method) to each category of investment (all subsidiaries, all associates, all JVs) but may choose different methods for different categories.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The scope distinction between NAS 27 and NFRS 10 is fundamental: NAS 27 governs how a parent accounts for its investments in its own books; NFRS 10 governs how a group presents its combined position. An entity that is not a parent but holds investments in associates (NAS 28) or joint ventures (NFRS 11) may also present separate financial statements under NAS 27.

The cost method's treatment of pre-acquisition dividends (<ParaRef std="IAS 27" para="12" />) is a practical concern: dividends received from pre-acquisition retained earnings should be deducted from the cost of the investment (return of capital, not income). However, distinguishing pre- from post-acquisition profits is complex when subsidiaries have long histories — many Nepali entities avoid this complexity by using the simplified approach (recognise all dividends as income unless there is clear evidence of return of capital). The equity method in separate financial statements, permitted since the 2014 amendment, uses NAS 28's mechanics applied to the separate FS — an option that provides information closer to consolidated statements.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

Investments in subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates are recognised in separate financial statements when the entity becomes a party to the contractual arrangement that creates the investment relationship. Under the cost method, the investment is recognised at acquisition cost. Under NFRS 9, it is recognised at fair value. Under the equity method, initial recognition is at cost and subsequently adjusted for post-acquisition changes in the investor's share of net assets. Dividends from investees are recognised in profit or loss when the right to receive payment is established (cost and NFRS 9 methods) or as a reduction of the carrying amount (equity method) (<ParaRef std="IAS 27" para="12" />).

## Measurement

Under the cost method, the investment is carried at cost less any accumulated impairment losses. No adjustment is made for the investee's profits, losses, or other comprehensive income. Under NFRS 9, the investment is measured at fair value with changes recognised either in profit or loss (FVTPL) or in other comprehensive income (FVOCI). Under the equity method, the carrying amount is adjusted for the investor's share of profit or loss and OCI of the investee, reduced by distributions received. The same measurement method must be applied to each category of investment (e.g., all subsidiaries measured at cost, all associates using equity method).

## Presentation & disclosure

When separate financial statements are prepared, the entity must disclose: a list of significant investments in subsidiaries, associates, and joint ventures (including name, principal place of business, proportion of ownership, and measurement method used), a description of the reasons for preparing separate financial statements if not also required to consolidate, and the identity of the financial statements produced in compliance with NFRS 10, NFRS 11, or NAS 28 to which they relate (<ParaRef std="IAS 27" para="16" />). If the parent applies the exemption from consolidation under NFRS 10, it must disclose that exemption and the identity of the entity producing consolidated statements for public use.
