## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

In Nepal, many businesses are owned by families or tightly knit groups of investors. A single family might own a bank, a trading house, and a manufacturing company. When these related companies do business with each other — say the trading house sells goods to the manufacturer at a special price — outsiders need to know about it. Otherwise, the companies could shift profits around or hide losses.

NAS 24 requires companies to disclose who their related parties are and what transactions they have done with them. For example, if the chairman of Himalayan Bank also owns a construction company that builds the bank's new headquarters, this relationship and the transaction must be disclosed.

**Why does this matter?** Related party transactions can affect profits, asset values, and risk. Investors and regulators need to see these dealings to judge whether the company is being run fairly.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 24 ensures that financial statements draw attention to the possibility that an entity's financial position and profit or loss may have been affected by the existence of related parties and by transactions and outstanding balances with such parties. Related parties include parent companies, subsidiaries, associates, joint ventures, key management personnel (KMP) and their close family members, and entities controlled or jointly controlled by KMP or their families.

Nepal's business landscape is characterised by concentrated ownership structures — promoter groups and family conglomerates often control multiple entities across banking, manufacturing, trading, and real estate. NRB's single obligor limits restrict lending to related parties by BFIs, while SEBON requires prior approval for material related party transactions by listed companies. NAS 24's disclosure requirements complement these regulatory controls by ensuring transparency in financial statements.

NAS 24 is fully aligned with IAS 24 — no carve-outs exist.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 24 operates as a disclosure standard — it does not prescribe accounting treatment for related party transactions but ensures transparency. The standard's definition of "related party" (<ParaRef std="IAS 24" para="9" />) is deliberately broad, capturing control-based, influence-based, and KMP-based relationships. In Nepal's concentrated ownership environment, the identification of related parties is often the most challenging aspect.

The "close family member" extension (<ParaRef std="IAS 24" para="9(a)(i)" />) — family members expected to influence or be influenced by a KMP — is particularly significant in Nepal where joint family structures mean business decisions are often influenced by extended family. The standard does not prescribe a definitive list of close family members, requiring entity-specific judgement. In practice, Nepali entities often under-disclose by limiting the definition to spouse and children, omitting siblings and parents who may exercise significant influence over business decisions.

The government-related entity exemption (<ParaRef std="IAS 24" para="25" />) provides partial relief from disclosure requirements where both the reporting entity and the transacting party are controlled by the same government. This is relevant for Nepal's state-owned enterprises — Nepal Telecom, Nepal Electricity Authority, Rastriya Banijya Bank — which transact extensively with each other and with government departments.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope & key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 24 applies to all entities and requires disclosure of:
- **Related party relationships** — even if no transactions occurred
- **Related party transactions** — any transfer of resources, services, or obligations between related parties, regardless of whether a price is charged

**Key terms:**
- **Related party** — a person or entity that is related to the reporting entity (e.g., parent, subsidiary, director, major shareholder).
- **Key management personnel (KMP)** — the people who plan, direct, and control the business — typically the board of directors and CEO/managing director.
- **Close family members** — family members who might influence or be influenced by the KMP in their dealings with the company (spouse, children, dependants).
- **Arm's length** — a transaction conducted as if the parties were unrelated, at fair market terms.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

The standard covers all entities in all financial statements. Related party relationships exist between parents and subsidiaries (regardless of transactions), entities with common control, associates, joint ventures, KMP of the entity or its parent, and entities controlled or significantly influenced by KMP or their close family members.

Key definitions:
- **Related party transaction** — a transfer of resources, services, or obligations between a reporting entity and a related party, regardless of whether a price is charged (<ParaRef std="IAS 24" para="9" />).
- **Key management personnel** — persons having authority and responsibility for planning, directing, and controlling the activities of the entity, directly or indirectly, including any director.
- **Compensation** — all employee benefits under NAS 19 and NFRS 2 (share-based payments), including short-term benefits, post-employment benefits, other long-term benefits, and termination benefits.
- **Government-related entity** — an entity that is controlled, jointly controlled, or significantly influenced by a government. Partial exemption from disclosure applies.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

Critical scope nuances for Nepal: (a) two entities are related if they are members of the same group (parent, subsidiary, fellow subsidiary) — relevant for Nepal's conglomerates like the Chaudhary Group, which spans food (Wai Wai), banking (Nabil), real estate, and hospitality; (b) post-employment benefit plans for the benefit of employees of the entity are related parties, capturing provident fund and gratuity trust transactions; (c) a person who has significant influence over the entity and a person who is KMP of a parent create indirect related party relationships.

The distinction between "arm's length" disclosure and "arm's length" assertion is important: NAS 24 (<ParaRef std="IAS 24" para="23" />) permits disclosure that terms are equivalent to arm's length only if it can be substantiated. Many Nepali entities routinely state "transactions are at arm's length" without supporting evidence — auditors should challenge unsubstantiated arm's length claims.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

NAS 24 is a disclosure standard — it does not establish recognition or measurement requirements for related party transactions. Transactions are accounted for in accordance with the applicable NFRS standards (e.g., revenue under NFRS 15, financial instruments under NFRS 9). However, the existence of related party relationships may affect the recognition assessment under other standards — for example, impairment testing of receivables from related parties, or fair value measurement where the only market evidence comes from related party transactions.

## Measurement

The standard does not prescribe how to measure related party transactions. Transactions should be measured in accordance with the requirements of the relevant standard. However, NAS 24 requires disclosure of whether transactions were made on terms equivalent to arm's length — and such claims can only be made if substantiated (<ParaRef std="IAS 24" para="23" />). For KMP compensation, the standard requires disclosure of total compensation in aggregate and by category: short-term employee benefits, post-employment benefits, other long-term benefits, termination benefits, and share-based payments.

## Presentation & disclosure

Disclosures required include the nature of the related party relationship, and for each category of related party transactions: the amount of transactions, outstanding balances (including commitments), terms and conditions, provisions for doubtful debts, and bad debt expense (<ParaRef std="IAS 24" para="18" />). Parent-subsidiary relationships must be disclosed even when there are no transactions. The name of the parent and the ultimate controlling party must be disclosed. KMP compensation is disclosed in total and by category. Government-related entities receive a partial exemption — individual transaction disclosures are not required, but the nature and amount of individually significant transactions must still be disclosed.
