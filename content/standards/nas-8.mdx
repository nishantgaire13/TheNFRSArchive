## Overview

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

Imagine you are cooking dal bhat and you decide to switch from mustard oil to sunflower oil. That is a change in your cooking "policy." Now imagine you estimated you had enough rice for 10 days but actually it lasted only 7 — that is a change in your "estimate." And if you realised you had been measuring rice with a broken cup all last month, that was an "error."

NAS 8 deals with exactly these three situations in accounting: **accounting policies** (the specific rules a company chooses to follow), **changes in estimates** (updating guesses as new information becomes available), and **prior-period errors** (mistakes found in previously published financial statements).

The key rule is straightforward: if a company changes its accounting policy or finds an error from a previous year, it must go back and restate the earlier numbers as if the new policy had always been used or the error had never occurred. But if it simply updates an estimate (like changing the useful life of a vehicle from 10 years to 8 years), it only changes the numbers going forward. This distinction — **retrospective** (going back) versus **prospective** (going forward) — is the heart of NAS 8.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 8 establishes a hierarchy for selecting accounting policies: first, apply the specific NFRS that addresses the transaction; if no standard directly applies, management uses judgment to develop a policy that results in relevant and faithfully represented information, considering (in descending order) requirements in NFRS dealing with similar issues, the Conceptual Framework's definitions and recognition criteria, and pronouncements of other standard-setting bodies.

The standard distinguishes three categories with different accounting treatments: **changes in accounting policy** are applied retrospectively (restating comparatives) unless impracticable; **changes in accounting estimates** are applied prospectively (current and future periods only); and **prior-period errors** are corrected retrospectively by restating comparatives. The distinction between a policy change and an estimate change can be subtle — the IASB clarified in 2021 amendments that a change in a measurement technique is an estimate change unless it results from adopting a new policy.

Nepal's 2018 NFRS transition generated massive NAS 8 activity: entities moving from Nepal Accounting Standards (old GAAP) to NFRS treated first-time adoption adjustments under NFRS 1, but subsequent changes — such as revising depreciation methods or correcting classification errors discovered during transition — fell under NAS 8.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

NAS 8 operationalises the Framework's qualitative characteristics of comparability and faithful representation through its retrospective/prospective distinction. The policy-versus-estimate boundary (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="32-38" />) is one of the most judgment-intensive areas in NFRS. The 2021 amendments to IAS 8 (Definition of Accounting Estimates) clarified that accounting estimates are "monetary amounts in financial statements that are subject to measurement uncertainty" — this clarification applies equally under NAS 8.

The impracticability exception (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="23-27" />) provides relief from retrospective application when the entity cannot determine the period-specific or cumulative effects of the change. In Nepal, impracticability claims are common among entities that lack robust historical data systems — particularly those that transitioned to NFRS with limited prior records. However, the threshold is high: the entity must demonstrate that it cannot determine the effects "after making every reasonable effort."

The interaction between NAS 8 and NAS 1 is critical: retrospective restatement under NAS 8 triggers the requirement for a third balance sheet at the beginning of the earliest comparative period presented (<ParaRef std="IAS 1" para="10(f)" />). Many Nepali entities overlook this third balance sheet, particularly during voluntary policy changes.

</DifficultyContent>

## Scope &amp; key definitions

<DifficultyContent level="beginner">

NAS 8 applies whenever a company needs to **choose** an accounting rule, **change** a rule or estimate, or **fix** a mistake in its past financial statements.

**Key terms:**
- **Accounting policy** — The specific rules and methods a company uses to prepare its accounts (e.g., depreciating buildings over 40 years using the straight-line method).
- **Change in accounting estimate** — An adjustment because of new information or experience (e.g., revising the useful life of a truck from 8 years to 6 years because of heavy usage on Nepal's mountain roads).
- **Prior-period error** — A mistake in past financial statements caused by not using information that was available at the time (e.g., forgetting to record an invoice from a supplier).
- **Retrospective application** — Applying a new policy as if it had always been used — going back and changing past numbers.
- **Prospective application** — Only changing numbers from now onward.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="medium">

NAS 8 applies to selecting and applying accounting policies, and accounting for changes in policies, changes in estimates, and corrections of prior-period errors (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="1" />). The tax effects of corrections and changes are accounted for under NAS 12.

Technical definitions:
- **Accounting policies** — The specific principles, bases, conventions, rules, and practices applied by an entity in preparing and presenting financial statements (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="5" />).
- **Change in accounting estimate** — An adjustment of the carrying amount of an asset or liability, or related expense, resulting from reassessing the expected future benefits and obligations associated with that asset or liability (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="5" />).
- **Prior-period errors** — Omissions from, and misstatements in, financial statements for one or more prior periods arising from failure to use, or misuse of, reliable information that was available and could reasonably be expected to have been obtained (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="5" />).
- **Impracticable** — Applying a requirement is impracticable when the entity cannot apply it after making every reasonable effort to do so.

</DifficultyContent>

<DifficultyContent level="pro">

The scope excludes the initial application of a policy to transactions not previously recognised — this is not a "change" in policy. The hierarchy for selecting policies in the absence of a specific NFRS (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="10-12" />) is frequently relevant in Nepal where novel transactions arise: for example, accounting for hydropower purchase agreements with Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) where no single standard may squarely apply, or accounting for cooperative society transactions not directly addressed by NFRS.

The interplay between NAS 8 and NFRS 1 (First-time Adoption) is critical: NFRS 1 governs the initial transition to NFRS and provides specific exemptions and exceptions. Once an entity has adopted NFRS, any subsequent change falls under NAS 8. Entities that adopted NFRS in 2018 and later discovered transition errors must determine whether the error relates to the NFRS 1 transition (adjust opening NFRS balance sheet) or to a subsequent period (restate under NAS 8). The line between "transition adjustment" and "post-transition error" has been contentious, particularly for BFIs adjusting NFRS 9 expected credit loss models.

</DifficultyContent>

## Recognition

Changes in accounting policies are recognised retrospectively: the entity adjusts the opening balance of each affected component of equity for the earliest prior period presented and restates comparative amounts as if the new policy had always been applied (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="22" />). If the cumulative effect at the beginning of the earliest period presented cannot be determined, the entity applies the new policy prospectively from the earliest date practicable.

Changes in accounting estimates are recognised prospectively in profit or loss in the period of the change and, if applicable, future periods. If the change affects assets or liabilities, it is recognised by adjusting the carrying amount of the related asset or liability in the period of the change.

Prior-period errors are corrected by restating the comparative amounts for the prior period(s) in which the error occurred, or if the error occurred before the earliest prior period presented, by restating the opening balances of assets, liabilities, and equity for the earliest prior period presented (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="42" />).

## Measurement

NAS 8 does not prescribe specific measurement bases — those are set by individual standards. However, the standard governs how measurement changes are treated: a change in measurement basis (e.g., from cost model to revaluation model for PPE under NAS 16) is a change in accounting policy and requires retrospective application unless the specific standard provides transitional provisions. A change in measurement technique (e.g., using a different valuation approach for fair value measurement) is a change in accounting estimate and is applied prospectively.

## Presentation &amp; disclosure

For changes in accounting policy: disclose the nature of the change, reasons for the change, the amount of the adjustment for each financial statement line item affected, and the amount of the adjustment relating to periods before those presented. For changes in estimates: disclose the nature and amount of the change, or state that the amount is impracticable to estimate. For prior-period errors: disclose the nature of the error, the amount of the correction for each financial statement line item, and the circumstances that led to the error's existence (<ParaRef std="IAS 8" para="49" />).

In Nepal, prior-period error disclosures are particularly common in the banking sector where NRB examination findings require BFIs to restate loan loss provisions. SEBON requires listed entities to separately disclose any restatement of previously published financial information in their annual reports.
